# Torah Reading / קְרִיאַת הַתּוֹרָה {#torah-reading}

## Overview

The public reading of the Torah (*keriat ha-Torah*) is performed from a handwritten parchment scroll (*sefer Torah*).  The gabbai is responsible for coordinating every aspect of the reading, from removing the scroll from the ark to its return.

This chapter follows the reading in the order of the service.  The laws behind it are collected in the two annexes at the back of the book: [Annex A: Laws of Aliyot](#annex-a) and [Annex B: Laws of the Gabbai](#annex-b).

## The Number of Aliyot  {#number-of-aliyot}

The number of Torah portions (*aliyot*, עֲלִיּוֹת) called varies by day. The following table documents the standard number of aliyot for the day/category.  It also notes (with +1) when the extra maftir/מַפְטִיר reading is added (for the person who will read the haftarah/הַפְטָרָה). 

<!-- TODO: when a gabbai may add aliyot beyond the standard number. -->

| Day                                  | Number of *Aliyot* |
| ------------------------------------ | :----------------: |
| Monday / Thursday / Shabbat Mincha   | 3                  |
| Shabbat Morning                      | 7 + 1              |
| Rosh Chodesh / Chol Ha-Moed          | 4                  |
| Rosh Chodesh on Shabbat              | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Tov (three Pilgrimage Festivals) | 5 + 1              |
| Yom Tov on Shabbat                   | 7 + 1              |
| Rosh Hashanah                        | 5 + 1              |
| Rosh Hashanah on Shabbat             | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur (Morning)                 | 6 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur (Afternoon)               | 2 + 1              |
| Fast Days (Morning)                  | 3                  |
| Fast Days (Afternoon)                | 2 + 1              |

In the table, "+ 1" is the maftir.  On Yom Kippur and fast-day Mincha, "2 + 1" means three aliyot in all: the third oleh is the maftir and reads the haftarah (see [Calling the Maftir](#maftir)).

NOTE: Fast Days refer to the public fasts: Tzom Gedaliah, Asarah b'Tevet, Ta'anit Esther, Shiv'ah Asar b'Tammuz and Tisha B'Av.

## Calling an *Oleh* {#calling-an-oleh}

When calling a person to the Torah, the gabbai announces him in Hebrew by his own Hebrew name and his father's Hebrew name, joined by *ben* ("son of").  Every call opens with *ya'amod* ("let him stand"); only the ending changes.  The calling formulas are collected in this section, and the sections that follow refer back to them.  The *bimkom Kohen* and *bimkom Levi* formulas are the one exception: they appear with the rules for when there is no Kohen or Levi, and are linked from here.  The magbi'ah and golel are not olim; the wording for calling them is under [Calling the Magbi'ah and Golel](#calling-hagbah-gelilah).  The formulas assume a male oleh; the congregation's rabbi should be asked about the wording for a *bat* where women are called.

Which name to use for a convert, or for someone whose father is unknown, is covered under [Names Used When Calling an Oleh](#names-in-announcement) in Annex A.  Learning the names before the service is one of the gabbai's duties (see [Knowing the Names](#knowing-the-names) in Annex B).

### Introductory Readings {#introductory-torah-readings}

Before the first call, the gabbai begins with the form customary for the day.  Congregations vary, and the following headings distinguish only the principal contexts; follow the congregation's established siddur and nusach.

#### Weekdays

On a weekday, the gabbai begins:[^tr38]

::: prayer
וְתִגָּלֶה וְתֵרָאֶה מַלְכוּתוֹ עָלֵינוּ בִּזְמַן קָרוֹב וְיָחוֹן פְּלֵיטָתֵנוּ וּפְלֵיטַת עַמּוֹ בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל לְחֵן וּלְחֶסֶד וּלְרַחֲמִים וּלְרָצוֹן וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.
:::

*Ve-tigaleh ve-teraeh malkhuto aleinu bi-zman karov ve-yachon pleitateinu u-fleitat amo Beit Yisrael le-chen u-le-chesed u-le-rachamim u-li-ratzon ve-nomar Amen.*

The gabbai continues:

::: prayer
הַכֹּל הָבוּ גֹּדֶל לֵאלֹהֵינוּ וּתְנוּ כָבוֹד לַתּוֹרָה.
:::

*Ha-kol havu godel le-Elokeinu u-tenu kavod la-Torah.*

The gabbai then calls the Kohen according to the formulas [below](#calling-numbered-aliyot).

#### Shabbat Morning

At Shabbat morning, begin with the Shabbat opening:[^tr39]

::: prayer-shabbat
וְיַעֲזֹר וְיָגֵן וְיוֹשִׁיעַ לְכָל הַחוֹסִים בּוֹ,
וְיִגְלֶה מַלְכוּתוֹ עָלֵינוּ בִּזְמַן קָרוֹב,
וְיִשְׁמַע אֶת כָּל עַמּוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל וְאֶת תְּפִלּוֹתֵינוּ יִשְׁמַע בְּרַחֲמִים וּלְרָצוֹן.

וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.
:::

*Ve-ya'azor ve-yagen ve-yoshi'a le-chol ha-chosim bo, ve-yigaleh malkhuto aleinu bi-zman karov, ve-yishma et kol amo Yisrael ve-et tefilloteinu bi-rachamim u-li-ratzon. Ve-nomar Amen.*

The gabbai continues:

::: prayer-shabbat
הַכֹּל הָבוּ גֹּדֶל לֵאלֹהֵינוּ וּתְנוּ כָבוֹד לַתּוֹרָה.
:::

*Ha-kol havu godel le-Elokeinu u-tenu kavod la-Torah.*

The gabbai then calls the Kohen according to the formulas [below](#calling-numbered-aliyot).

#### Shabbat Afternoon

At Shabbat *Mincha*, the gabbai begins:[^tr40]

::: prayer-shabbat
וְתִגָּלֶה וְתֵרָאֶה מַלְכוּתוֹ עָלֵינוּ בִּזְמַן קָרוֹב.

וְיָחוֹן פְּלֵיטָתֵנוּ וּפְלֵיטַת עַמּוֹ בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל לְחֵן וּלְחֶסֶד וּלְרַחֲמִים וּלְרָצוֹן וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.

הַכֹּל הָבוּ גֹּדֶל לֵאלֹהֵינוּ וּתְנוּ כָבוֹד לַתּוֹרָה.
:::

*Ve-tigaleh ve-teraeh malkhuto aleinu bi-zman karov. Ve-yachon pleitateinu u-fleitat amo Beit Yisrael le-chen u-le-chesed u-le-rachamim u-li-ratzon ve-nomar Amen. Ha-kol havu godel le-Elokeinu u-tenu kavod la-Torah.*

The gabbai then calls the Kohen according to the formulas [below](#calling-numbered-aliyot).

#### Festivals

On Yom Tov and Chol HaMoed, Siddur Ashkenaz uses the weekday introductory reading. [^tr38][^tr41]

::: prayer-chaggim
וְתִגָּלֶה וְתֵרָאֶה מַלְכוּתוֹ עָלֵינוּ בִּזְמַן קָרוֹב וְיָחוֹן פְּלֵיטָתֵנוּ וּפְלֵיטַת עַמּוֹ בֵּית יִשְׂרָאֵל לְחֵן וּלְחֶסֶד וּלְרַחֲמִים וּלְרָצוֹן וְנֹאמַר אָמֵן.

הַכֹּל הָבוּ גֹּדֶל לֵאלֹהֵינוּ וּתְנוּ כָבוֹד לַתּוֹרָה.
:::

*Ve-tigaleh ve-teraeh malkhuto aleinu bi-zman karov ve-yachon pleitateinu u-fleitat amo Beit Yisrael le-chen u-le-chesed u-le-rachamim u-li-ratzon ve-nomar Amen. Ha-kol havu godel le-Elokeinu u-tenu kavod la-Torah.*

The gabbai then calls the Kohen according to the formulas [below](#calling-numbered-aliyot).

### After the Call

After calling the oleh by name, the gabbai says:

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ שֶׁנָּתַן תּוֹרָה לְעַמּוֹ יִשְׂרָאֵל בִּקְדֻשָּׁתוֹ.
:::

*Baruch she-natan Torah le-amo Yisrael bi-kdushato.*

The congregation answers:

::: prayer
וְאַתֶּם הַדְּבֵקִים בַּיהוה אֱלֹהֵיכֶם חַיִּים כֻּלְּכֶם הַיּוֹם.
:::

*Ve-attem ha-devekim ba-Adonai Eloheichem chayim kulchem ha-yom.*

### The Aliyot {#calling-numbered-aliyot}

The first aliyah is announced by the oleh's status as a Kohen, with no number:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַכֹּהֵן
:::

The second is announced by his status as a Levi:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַלֵּוִי
:::

From the third aliyah onward, the gabbai announces the number of the aliyah:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] [שְׁלִישִׁי / רְבִיעִי / חֲמִישִׁי / שִׁשִּׁי / שְׁבִיעִי]
:::

If a Kohen or Levi is called for a later aliyah (for example, *acharon* or maftir), the gabbai adds *ha-Kohen* or *ha-Levi* after his name, before the number.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Many Sephardic communities use the formula *ya'amod* followed only by the person's Hebrew name, without announcing the ordinal number of the aliyah.  The aliyah number is considered already known to the congregation.  Some communities also add the phrase *v'yevarech* ("and may he bless").[^tr1]
:::

### In Place of a Kohen or Levi (*Bimkom*)

When there is no Kohen, the first oleh is announced *bimkom Kohen* ("in place of a Kohen"); when there is no Levi, the Kohen is called again *bimkom Levi*.  The wording is given with the rules for each case: see [No Kohen Present](#no-kohen-present) and [No Levi Present](#no-levi-present).

### Maftir {#calling-maftir}

The maftir is called with the word *maftir* in place of the aliyah number:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] מַפְטִיר
:::

If the maftir is a Kohen or Levi, add *ha-Kohen* or *ha-Levi* after his name, as for any other aliyah.

### A Bar Mitzvah {#calling-bar-mitzvah}

For a *bar mitzvah* boy many congregations add *ha-bachur ha-bar mitzvah* before his name.  Called for maftir, he is announced:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד הַבָּחוּר הַבַּר מִצְוָה [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] מַפְטִיר
:::

## When a Kohen or Levi Isn't Present {#no-kohen-or-levi}

Normally the first aliyah goes to a *Kohen* and the second to a *Levi* (see [Kohen, Levi, and Yisrael](#kohen-levi-yisrael) in Annex A).  Before the Torah is taken out, the gabbai should look around the room to see who is there, so the first two aliyot can be planned before the reading starts.

### No Kohen Present {#no-kohen-present}

If there is no Kohen in the synagogue, the usual order no longer applies (*nitperdah ha-chavilah*, "the bundle has come apart").  A *Yisrael* is called for the first aliyah *in place of a Kohen* (*bimkom Kohen*).[^tr2]  A Levi may be called first instead, unless a Yisrael of greater standing is present, in which case the Yisrael is called.[^tr3]  Whoever is called, the gabbai announces that he is coming up *bimkom Kohen*, so that no one mistakes him for a Kohen.[^tr4]

A Yisrael is announced:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] בִּמְקוֹם כֹּהֵן
:::

A Levi is announced with *ha-Levi* after his name, as usual, followed by *bimkom Kohen*:[^tr5]

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַלֵּוִי בִּמְקוֹם כֹּהֵן
:::

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** The Shulchan Aruch, following the Rambam, does not call a Levi at all when there is no Kohen, not even for the first aliyah.  Calling a Levi first comes from the *Rama*'s gloss.  Communities that follow the Shulchan Aruch therefore call a Yisrael *bimkom Kohen* and do not use the Levi formula above.  Follow the established custom of the congregation.[^tr6]
:::

Many gabbaim first say *ein kan Kohen* (אֵין כָּאן כֹּהֵן, "there is no Kohen here").  This is a common custom, not a requirement.[^tr7]

Once the first aliyah has gone to someone other than a Kohen, **a Levi is not called for the second aliyah**.  Otherwise people, especially anyone who arrived after the announcement, would assume the first oleh was a Kohen.  The second aliyah goes to a Yisrael and is announced with the number *sheni* (שֵׁנִי).[^tr8]  In the same way, if a Levi was called first, another Levi must not be called second.[^tr9]  Authorities disagree about calling a Levi for a later aliyah (third or fourth) after a Yisrael was called *bimkom Kohen*.  Where the custom is to call Kohanim and Leviim only for *acharon* or *maftir*, the question doesn't come up.[^tr3]

### No Levi Present {#no-levi-present}

If a Kohen is present but there is no Levi, **the Kohen who received the first aliyah is called again for the second**, *in place of a Levi* (*bimkom Levi*).  He recites both blessings again.[^tr10]  He is announced *bimkom Levi*:[^tr11]

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַכֹּהֵן בִּמְקוֹם לֵוִי
:::

A different Kohen is never called for the second aliyah, and a Yisrael is not called in place of the Levi.  Either would suggest that the first Kohen is not a proper Kohen.[^tr12]  This applies even on a fast day when the Levi who is present is not fasting.  The Kohen repeats *bimkom Levi* rather than a Yisrael being called.[^tr13]  The third aliyah onward goes to Yisraelim as usual.

### A Kohen Who Is Present but Unavailable

If the only Kohen present is reciting *Keriat Shema*, its blessings, or the *Amidah*, and may not interrupt, a Yisrael is called in his place.  The gabbai does not wait for him, and does not even delay taking out the Torah.[^tr14]  If the Kohen is between paragraphs (*bein ha-perakim*) and there is no other Kohen, he may be called.[^tr15]  When a Yisrael (or a Levi) is called in place of a Kohen who is in the room, the gabbai announces him *bimkom Kohen* as usual but does not say *ein kan Kohen*, since there is a Kohen here.[^tr16]

On the fasts that follow Pesach and Sukkot (the *Vayechal* fasts), a Kohen who is not fasting is not called.  A Yisrael is called instead, and ideally the Kohen leaves the room so no one wonders why he was passed over.[^tr17]  On the other public fasts the rule is firmer: a Kohen who is not fasting should leave the synagogue, because everyone knows that nearly everyone fasts on those days, and people would assume that he was passed over for being disqualified.[^tr17a]  If another Kohen who is fasting is present, the first Kohen need not leave, but he should tell the *shaliach tzibbur* not to call him.[^tr17b]  A Levi who is not fasting is likewise not called; see [No Levi Present](#no-levi-present).

If the gabbai wants to give the first aliyah to someone else while a Kohen is in the room, the Kohen must first waive his right or leave the room (see [Kohen, Levi, and Yisrael](#kohen-levi-yisrael) in Annex A).

### A Kohen Who Arrives Late

If a Kohen walks in after a Yisrael was called for the first aliyah:

- **If the Yisrael has already begun the blessing** (he has said *Baruch atah Hashem*), he continues.  His aliyah counts, and the Kohen is not called after him.[^tr18]
- **If only *Barchu* has been said**, the Yisrael steps aside and waits at the bimah while the Kohen and then a Levi are called.  The Yisrael then takes his aliyah.[^tr19]

### A Kohen or Levi Called Who Isn't There

If the gabbai calls a Kohen or Levi by name and he turns out not to be present, the gabbai does not call another Kohen (or Levi) by name, because that would suggest something is wrong with the first one.  The custom is for another Kohen or Levi to come up on his own.[^tr20]

### A Congregation of Only Kohanim (or Leviim)

If everyone present is a Kohen, Kohanim are called one after another.  No one draws any conclusion from it, since everyone knows only Kohanim are there.  If there is a single Yisrael among them, he is called first for the sake of peace (*darkhei shalom*).  The same applies to a congregation made up entirely of Leviim.[^tr21]  The *Kitzur Shulchan Aruch* advises such a congregation to ask a rabbi how to proceed.[^tr22]

### Summary

| Who is present         | First aliyah                      | Second aliyah             |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Kohen and Levi         | Kohen                             | Levi                      |
| Kohen, no Levi         | Kohen                             | Same Kohen, *bimkom Levi* |
| Levi, no Kohen         | Yisrael or Levi, *bimkom Kohen*   | Yisrael (not a Levi)      |
| Neither Kohen nor Levi | Yisrael, *bimkom Kohen*           | Yisrael                   |

## Calling the Maftir (מַפְטִיר) {#maftir}

The *maftir* is the person called to the final, additional reading, who then reads (or chants) the *haftarah*/הַפְטָרָה from the Prophets.  For who may receive maftir and who customarily does, see [Laws of Maftir](#laws-of-maftir) in Annex A.

### When There Is a Maftir {#when-there-is-a-maftir}

A separate maftir is called on Shabbat morning, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur morning, i.e., whenever a haftarah follows the reading.  At *Mincha* on a public fast day (see the list under [The Number of Aliyot](#number-of-aliyot)), at Yom Kippur *Mincha*, and on Tisha B'Av morning, there is no extra aliyah.  The third oleh is the maftir and reads the haftarah after his aliyah.[^tr23]

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** The haftarah at fast-day *Mincha* comes from the *Rama*'s gloss.  The Shulchan Aruch itself mentions only the Torah reading.  Many Sephardic communities therefore read no haftarah at *Mincha* on an ordinary fast day.  At such a reading the third oleh is not a maftir.  Follow the established custom of the congregation.
:::

### Before Calling the Maftir

1. **Confirm the maftir before the service.**  Make sure he knows which haftarah is being read (watch for special haftarot on Rosh Chodesh, the Four Parshiyot, and other special Shabbatot) and that he is prepared to read it and say its blessings.
2. **Half-Kaddish (*chatzi kaddish*).**  After the last required aliyah ends, the reader says half-Kaddish, and only then is the maftir called.  Because the maftir is not one of the required aliyot, the Kaddish marks the end of the obligatory reading.[^tr24]  The text is in [Half Kaddish](#half-kaddish) in the [Kaddishim](#kaddishim) chapter.
3. **When a second scroll is used.**  On Yom Tov, Shabbat Rosh Chodesh, Shabbat Chanukah, and the Four Parshiyot, the maftir is read from a second *sefer Torah*.[^tr25]  When three scrolls are used, the maftir reads from the last one.[^tr26]  For which days need two or three scrolls, and the order of kaddish, hagbah and gelilah with each, see [When More Than One Torah Is Read](#multiple-scrolls) below.
4. **Call the maftir.**  For the wording, see [Maftir](#calling-maftir) and [A Bar Mitzvah](#calling-bar-mitzvah) under Calling an Oleh.

### After the Maftir Reading {#after-maftir}

After the maftir reading, the second (or only) scroll is lifted and wrapped (see [Hagbah and Gelilah](#hagbah-gelilah) below).  The maftir does not begin the haftarah until the wrapping is finished, so that the golel can also listen.[^tr27]

For the text of the blessings before and after the haftarah, and which form to use on each day, see the [Haftarah Blessings](#haftarah-blessings) chapter.

For the full Torah blessings said by each oleh, and for the blessing of *Birkat HaGomel*, see [Torah and Gomel Blessings](#torah-and-gomel-blessings).

## Hagbah and Gelilah {#hagbah-gelilah}

After the final aliyah (including maftir), two more people are called to the bimah.  *Hagbah* (הַגְבָּהָה) is lifting the open scroll to show it to the congregation; the person given this honour is the *magbi'ah* (מַגְבִּיהַּ).  *Gelilah* (גְּלִילָה) is rolling the scroll closed, fastening the belt (*gartel*), and putting the mantle and ornaments back on; the person given this honour is the *golel* (גּוֹלֵל).

### Calling the Magbi'ah and Golel {#calling-hagbah-gelilah}

At the point where hagbah is done (see the Sephardic custom below), the gabbai calls one person for hagbah and another for gelilah:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לְהַגְבָּהָה

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לִגְלִילָה
:::

The gabbai usually calls both people together, so the golel is already at the bimah when the scroll is lifted:

::: prayer
יַעַמְדוּ הַמַּגְבִּיהַ וְהַגּוֹלֵל
:::

If the congregation recites a *Mi Shebeirach* for them, see *Hagbah & Gelilah* in the [Mi Shebeirach](#mi-shebeirach) chapter.

When more than one scroll is read, each scroll has its own magbi'ah and golel (see [When More Than One Torah Is Read](#multiple-scrolls)).  When calling hagbah and gelilah for a second or third scroll, some gabbaim name which scroll it is so there is no confusion:

::: prayer
יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לְהַגְבָּהַת סֵפֶר תּוֹרָה שֵׁנִי

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לִגְלִילַת סֵפֶר תּוֹרָה שֵׁנִי
:::

::: prayer
 יַעַמְדוּ הַמַּגְבִּיהַ וְהַגּוֹלֵל סֵפֶר שֵׁנִי
:::

For the third scroll, say שְׁלִישִׁי in place of שֵׁנִי.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** In Sephardic communities (and some others) hagbah is done *before* the reading, right after the scroll is placed on the bimah, rather than after it.   The gabbai calls the honour at that point instead of after maftir, so after the maftir reading only the wrapping and the return of the scroll remain before the haftarah begins.[^tr28]
:::

### Hagbah (הַגְבָּהָה)

The magbi'ah opens the scroll about three columns wide (more if he can lift it safely).  He lifts it high and turns to his right and left, and to the front and back, so that the whole congregation can see the writing.[^tr29]  The congregation stands and says:

::: prayer
וְזֹאת הַתּוֹרָה אֲשֶׁר שָׂם מֹשֶׁה לִפְנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל עַל פִּי ה' בְּיַד מֹשֶׁה
:::

Lifting a full, open scroll takes strength and practice.  The gabbai should only give hagbah to someone who can do it safely and is comfortable doing it.  The gabbai should not give hagbah to someone whose hands shake badly, or to someone elderly or weak who would have to sit down right away instead of holding the scroll up long enough for the congregation to see it.  Such a person should also decline the honour himself.[^tr30]  When the scroll is heavy or most of the parchment is wound on one side (for example, in Bereishit or late in Devarim), the gabbai should choose someone with experience.

The magbi'ah then sits down holding the scroll, and the golel rolls it closed.

NOTE: The gabbai should have the chair ready behind the magbi'ah while he is lifting.  

::: note-chabad
**Chabad custom:** Hagbah is done after the reading, as in Ashkenazic communities, but the scroll is lifted twice: first open and then closed, so that the honour is done according to every opinion.  The magbi'ah:

1. Opens the scroll a little less than three columns.  He stands with his feet together and lowers the scroll over the front edge of the bimah until about a third of it extends beyond it.
2. Presses down and lifts, opening it a little more in the air so that three columns show.
3. Without moving his feet, turns the scroll to the right, to the left, and to the right again.
4. Puts the scroll back down on the bimah and closes it.  He does **not** sit down while the scroll is still open.
5. Steps back and sits down holding the closed scroll for gelilah.

The congregation does not point at the scroll with a finger.  Following the Arizal,[^tr30a] each person tries to look at the writing from close by and find a letter of his own name (this and the no-pointing rule are not stated for all Ashkenazim).

The Rebbe insisted on putting the scroll back on the bimah between hagbah and gelilah.[^tr30b]  Because this order leaves the scroll open at the edge of the bimah, give it only to someone strong and experienced (see above).  The usual Ashkenazic order, sitting with the open scroll, remains correct for other communities; this box does not replace it.
:::

### Gelilah (גְּלִילָה)

The golel rolls the scroll closed and dresses it: the belt (*gartel*), the mantle, and then the breastplate, pointer (*yad*) and crown as the community does it.  The scroll should be rolled tight and closed so that the two rollers meet at a seam between two sheets of parchment.  That way, if it ever tears, it tears at the seam.[^tr31]  The Talmud teaches that the golel receives a reward equal to that of all who read from the Torah.[^tr32]

NOTE: Today one person lifts and another rolls, so the magbi'ah is considered the main golel.[^tr33]  This is one reason many communities treat hagbah as the more important of the two honours.

In many communities gelilah is given to a boy under bar mitzvah, to train him in mitzvot (*chinuch*).  The boy should be old enough to understand that he is handling something holy.[^tr30]  An adult should stand nearby to help, especially with a heavy scroll.

## When More Than One Torah Is Read {#multiple-scrolls}

On some days the reading comes from two or three different places in the Torah.  The scroll is not rolled from one place to another in front of the congregation, out of respect for the congregation (*kevod ha-tzibbur*).  So a separate scroll is prepared for each reading.  If the community has only one kosher scroll (for example, a small minyan, or a second scroll found to be *pasul*), the one scroll is rolled to the second reading in front of the congregation.[^tr34]  Each scroll gets its own hagbah and its own golel, so the gabbai needs to arrange **two or three pairs** of people before the service.

| Number of Scrolls | Days                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| :---------------: | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2                 | Yom Tov (Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret); Rosh Hashanah; Yom Kippur morning; Shabbat Chol Ha-Moed; Chol Ha-Moed Pesach (weekdays); Shabbat Rosh Chodesh; Shabbat Chanukah; Rosh Chodesh Tevet (weekday, during Chanukah); Shabbat Zachor and Shabbat Parah; Shabbat Shekalim and Shabbat Ha-Chodesh (when not on Rosh Chodesh) |
| 3                 | Simchat Torah; Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Tevet (Chanukah); Shekalim on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Adar; Ha-Chodesh on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Nissan                                                |

Note that weekday Chol Ha-Moed **Sukkot** uses only one scroll, unlike Chol Ha-Moed Pesach.

**Prepare every scroll before the service.**  Each scroll should be rolled to its reading ahead of time, and it should be clearly marked which scroll is first, second and third.  The gabbai should check this before the service, since a wrong scroll means rolling in front of the congregation.  He should also tell each magbi'ah and golel *before the service* which scroll is theirs.  This helps the reading go smoothly.

### Order with two scrolls

1. The main aliyot are read from the first scroll.
2. The second scroll is brought and placed on the bimah next to the first, already rolled to its reading.  The first scroll is not taken away until the second is on the bimah, so that no one's attention is taken off the mitzvah.[^tr35]
3. Half-kaddish is recited, with both scrolls on the bimah.[^tr36]
4. Hagbah and gelilah of the **first** scroll.  The magbi'ah sits holding it.  The second scroll is not opened, and its cover is not removed, until the first has been rolled.[^tr37]
5. Maftir (or the extra reading) is read from the second scroll.
6. Hagbah and gelilah of the **second** scroll.  Then the haftarah, once the golel has finished (see [After the Maftir Reading](#after-maftir)).

### Order with three scrolls

1. The main aliyot are read from the first scroll.
2. The second scroll is placed on the bimah.  **No kaddish is said yet.**
3. Hagbah and gelilah of the first scroll.
4. The next aliyah is read from the second scroll.
5. The third scroll is placed on the bimah next to the second, and half-kaddish is recited over these two.  The first scroll does not need to be brought back.[^tr36]
6. Hagbah and gelilah of the second scroll.
7. Maftir is read from the third scroll.
8. Hagbah and gelilah of the third scroll.  Then the haftarah.

On Simchat Torah, the order is often different, depending on local custom (many aliyot from the first scroll, *Chatan Torah*, *Chatan Bereishit* and maftir).  The gabbai should agree the plan with the rabbi ahead of time.

## Correcting Errors {#correcting-errors}

The gabbai must follow the reading carefully in a *tikkun* (a printed book with both the vowelised and unvowelised text side by side) and correct the reader (*ba'al korei*) when a significant error (*taut me'akev*) occurs.[^tr42]

An error is significant (*me'akev*) if it changes the meaning of the text.  The gabbai must know the text well enough to tell such an error from a minor mispronunciation.  Minor mispronunciations that do not affect meaning need not be corrected publicly, though the gabbai may quietly guide the reader.
