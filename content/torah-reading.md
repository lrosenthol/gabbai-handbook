# Torah Reading / קְרִיאַת הַתּוֹרָה

## Overview

The public reading of the Torah (*keriat ha-Torah*) is performed from a handwritten parchment scroll (*sefer Torah*).  The gabbai is responsible for coordinating every aspect of the reading, from removing the scroll from the ark to its return.

## The Number of Aliyot (עֲלִיּוֹת)

The number of Torah portions (*aliyot*) called varies by day. The following table documents the standard number of aliyot for the day/category.  It also notes (with +1) when the extra maftir/מַפְטִיר reading is added (for the person who will read the haftarah/הַפְטָרָה).  A gabbai may also call additional aliyot under certain circumstances ([TBD]).

| ------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Shabbat Morning                      | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur Morning                   | 6 + 1              |
| Yom Tov (three Pilgrimage Festivals) | 5                  |
| Rosh Hashanah                        | 5                  |
| Rosh Chodesh / Chol Ha-Moed          | 4                  |
| Monday / Thursday / Shabbat Mincha   | 3                  |
| Yom Kippur Mincha                    | 2 + 1              |

## Calling an *Oleh* 

When calling a person to the Torah, the gabbai announces the *aliyah* in Hebrew.  The traditional formula includes the person's Hebrew name and father's name (*ben* for a man, *bat* for a woman):

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לַעֲלִיָּה [מִסְפַּר]

The gabbai should know in advance the Hebrew names of those receiving honours.  It is good practice to confirm names before the service.

## When a Kohen or Levi Isn't Present

Normally the first aliyah goes to a *Kohen* and the second to a *Levi* (see *Laws of Aliyot*).  Before the Torah is taken out, the gabbai should look around the room to see who is there, so the first two aliyot can be planned before the reading starts.

### No Kohen Present

If there is no Kohen in the synagogue, the usual order no longer applies (*nitperdah ha-chavilah*, "the bundle has come apart").  A *Yisrael* (or a Levi) is called for the first aliyah *in place of a Kohen* (*bimkom Kohen*).[^tr1]  The gabbai announces that no Kohen is present before calling him:

אֵין כָּאן כֹּהֵן, יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] בִּמְקוֹם כֹּהֵן

Once the first aliyah has gone to someone other than a Kohen, **a Levi is not called for the second aliyah**.  The second aliyah goes to a Yisrael and is announced as *sheni* in the usual way.[^tr2]  Most communities call a Yisrael, not a Levi, *bimkom Kohen*.  If a Levi is called first, then another Levi must not be called second.

### No Levi Present

If a Kohen is present but there is no Levi, **the Kohen who received the first aliyah is called again for the second**, *in place of a Levi* (*bimkom Levi*).  He recites both blessings again.[^tr3]  The gabbai calls him with:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַכֹּהֵן בִּמְקוֹם לֵוִי

A different Kohen is never called for the second aliyah, and a Yisrael is not called in place of the Levi.  The third aliyah onward goes to Yisraelim as usual.

### A Kohen Who Is Present but Unavailable

If the only Kohen present is in the middle of a part of the prayers where he may not interrupt (such as *Keriat Shema* or the *Amidah*), the gabbai does not wait for him.  He is treated as if he were not there and a Yisrael is called in his place.[^tr4]  Likewise, if the gabbai wants to give the first aliyah to someone else while a Kohen is in the room, the Kohen must first waive his right or leave the room (see *Laws of Aliyot*).

### Summary

| Who is present          | First aliyah                    | Second aliyah                    |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| Kohen and Levi          | Kohen                           | Levi                             |
| Kohen, no Levi          | Kohen                           | Same Kohen, *bimkom Levi*        |
| Levi, no Kohen          | Yisrael (or Levi), *bimkom Kohen* | Yisrael (not a Levi)           |
| Neither Kohen nor Levi  | Yisrael, *bimkom Kohen*         | Yisrael                          |

[^tr1]: Shulchan Aruch, *Orach Chayyim* 135:6; Talmud Bavli, *Gittin* 59b.
[^tr2]: Shulchan Aruch, *Orach Chayyim* 135:6, and *Mishnah Berurah* ad loc.
[^tr3]: Shulchan Aruch, *Orach Chayyim* 135:8.
[^tr4]: Shulchan Aruch, *Orach Chayyim* 135:5, and *Mishnah Berurah* ad loc.

## Correcting Errors

The gabbai must follow the reading carefully in a *tikkun* (a printed book with both the vowelised and unvowelised text side by side) and correct any significant error (*taut*) by the reader (*ba'al korei*).

An error is considered significant (*me'akev*) if it changes the meaning of the text.  Minor mispronunciations that do not affect meaning need not be corrected publicly, though the gabbai may quietly guide the reader.
