# Torah Reading / קְרִיאַת הַתּוֹרָה

## Overview

The public reading of the Torah (*keriat ha-Torah*) is performed from a handwritten parchment scroll (*sefer Torah*).  The gabbai is responsible for coordinating every aspect of the reading, from removing the scroll from the ark to its return.

## The Number of Aliyot (עֲלִיּוֹת)

The number of Torah portions (*aliyot*) called varies by day. The following table documents the standard number of aliyot for the day/category.  It also notes (with +1) when the extra maftir/מַפְטִיר reading is added (for the person who will read the haftarah/הַפְטָרָה).  A gabbai may also call additional aliyot under certain circumstances ([TBD]).

| Day                                  | Number of *Aliyot* |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Monday / Thursday / Shabbat Mincha   | 3                  |
| Shabbat Morning                      | 7 + 1              |
| Rosh Chodesh / Chol Ha-Moed          | 4                  |
| Rosh Chodesh on Shabbat              | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Tov (three Pilgrimage Festivals) | 5 + 1              |
| Yom Tov on Shabbat                   | 7 + 1              |
| Rosh Hashanah                        | 5 + 1              |
| Rosh Hashanah on Shabbat             | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur (Morning)                 | 6 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur (Afternoon)               | 2 + 1              |
| Fast Days (Morning)                  | 3                  |
| Fast Days (Afternoon)                | 2 + 1              |

NOTE: Fast Days refer to the public fasts: Tzom Gedaliah, Asarah b'Tevet, Ta'anit Esther, Shiv'ah Asar b'Tammuz and Tisha B'Av.

## Calling an *Oleh* 

When calling a person to the Torah, the gabbai announces the *aliyah* in Hebrew.  The traditional formula includes the person's Hebrew name and father's name (*ben* for a man, *bat* for a woman):

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לַעֲלִיָּה [מִסְפַּר]

The gabbai should know in advance the Hebrew names of those receiving honours.  It is good practice to confirm names before the service.

## When a Kohen or Levi Isn't Present

Normally the first aliyah goes to a *Kohen* and the second to a *Levi* (see *Laws of Aliyot*).  Before the Torah is taken out, the gabbai should look around the room to see who is there, so the first two aliyot can be planned before the reading starts.

### No Kohen Present

If there is no Kohen in the synagogue, the usual order no longer applies (*nitperdah ha-chavilah*, "the bundle has come apart").  A *Yisrael* is called for the first aliyah *in place of a Kohen* (*bimkom Kohen*).[^tr1]  A Levi may be called first instead, unless a Yisrael of greater standing is present, in which case the Yisrael is called.[^tr2]  Whoever is called, the gabbai announces that he is coming up *bimkom Kohen*, so that no one mistakes him for a Kohen:[^tr3]

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] בִּמְקוֹם כֹּהֵן

Many gabbaim first say *ein kan Kohen* (אֵין כָּאן כֹּהֵן, "there is no Kohen here").  This is a common custom, not a requirement.

Once the first aliyah has gone to someone other than a Kohen, **a Levi is not called for the second aliyah**.  Otherwise people, especially anyone who arrived after the announcement, would assume the first oleh was a Kohen.  The second aliyah goes to a Yisrael and is announced as *sheni* in the usual way.[^tr4]  In the same way, if a Levi was called first, another Levi must not be called second.[^tr5]  Authorities disagree about calling a Levi for a later aliyah (third or fourth) after a Yisrael was called *bimkom Kohen*.  Where the custom is to call Kohanim and Leviim only for *acharon* or *maftir*, the question doesn't come up.[^tr2]

### No Levi Present

If a Kohen is present but there is no Levi, **the Kohen who received the first aliyah is called again for the second**, *in place of a Levi* (*bimkom Levi*).  He recites both blessings again.[^tr6]  The gabbai calls him with:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַכֹּהֵן בִּמְקוֹם לֵוִי

A different Kohen is never called for the second aliyah, and a Yisrael is not called in place of the Levi.  Either would suggest that the first Kohen is not a proper Kohen.[^tr7]  This applies even on a fast day when the Levi who is present is not fasting.  The Kohen repeats *bimkom Levi* rather than a Yisrael being called.[^tr8]  The third aliyah onward goes to Yisraelim as usual.

### A Kohen Who Is Present but Unavailable

If the only Kohen present is reciting *Keriat Shema*, its blessings, or the *Amidah*, and may not interrupt, a Yisrael is called in his place.  The gabbai does not wait for him, and does not even delay taking out the Torah.[^tr9]  If the Kohen is between paragraphs (*bein ha-perakim*) and there is no other Kohen, he may be called.[^tr10]

On a public fast day, a Kohen who is not fasting is not called.  A Yisrael is called instead, and ideally the Kohen leaves the room so no one wonders why he was passed over.[^tr11]

If the gabbai wants to give the first aliyah to someone else while a Kohen is in the room, the Kohen must first waive his right or leave the room (see *Laws of Aliyot*).

### A Kohen Who Arrives Late

If a Kohen walks in after a Yisrael was called for the first aliyah:

- **If the Yisrael has already begun the blessing** (he has said *Baruch atah Hashem*), he continues.  His aliyah counts, and the Kohen is not called after him.[^tr12]
- **If only *Barchu* has been said**, the Yisrael steps aside and waits at the bimah while the Kohen and then a Levi are called.  The Yisrael then takes his aliyah.[^tr13]

### A Kohen or Levi Called Who Isn't There

If the gabbai calls a Kohen or Levi by name and he turns out not to be present, the gabbai does not call another Kohen (or Levi) by name, because that would suggest something is wrong with the first one.  The custom is for another Kohen or Levi to come up on his own.[^tr14]

### A Congregation of Only Kohanim (or Leviim)

If everyone present is a Kohen, Kohanim are called one after another.  No one draws any conclusion from it, since everyone knows only Kohanim are there.  If there is a single Yisrael among them, he is called first for the sake of peace (*darkhei shalom*).  The same applies to a congregation made up entirely of Leviim.[^tr15]

### Summary

| Who is present         | First aliyah                      | Second aliyah             |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Kohen and Levi         | Kohen                             | Levi                      |
| Kohen, no Levi         | Kohen                             | Same Kohen, *bimkom Levi* |
| Levi, no Kohen         | Yisrael or Levi, *bimkom Kohen*   | Yisrael (not a Levi)      |
| Neither Kohen nor Levi | Yisrael, *bimkom Kohen*           | Yisrael                   |

## Calling the Maftir (מַפְטִיר)

The *maftir* is the person called to the final, additional reading, who then reads (or chants) the *haftarah*/הַפְטָרָה from the Prophets.  For the laws of who may receive maftir, see *Maftir* under Laws of Aliyot.

### When There Is a Maftir

A separate maftir is called on Shabbat morning, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur morning, i.e., whenever a haftarah follows the reading.  At *Mincha* on a public fast day (Tzom Gedaliah, Asarah b'Tevet, Ta'anit Esther, Shiv'ah Asar b'Tammuz, and Tisha B'Av), at Yom Kippur *Mincha*, and on Tisha B'Av morning, there is no extra aliyah.  The third oleh is the maftir and reads the haftarah after his aliyah.[^tr16]

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** The haftarah at fast-day *Mincha* comes from the *Rama*'s gloss.  The Shulchan Aruch itself mentions only the Torah reading.  Many Sephardic communities therefore read no haftarah at *Mincha* on an ordinary fast day.  At such a reading the third oleh is not a maftir.  Follow the established custom of the congregation.
:::

### Before Calling the Maftir

1. **Confirm the maftir before the service.**  Make sure he knows which haftarah is being read (watch for special haftarot on Rosh Chodesh, the Four Parshiyot, and other special Shabbatot) and that he is prepared to read it and say its blessings.
2. **Half-Kaddish (*chatzi kaddish*).**  After the last required aliyah ends, the reader says half-Kaddish, and only then is the maftir called.  Because the maftir is not one of the required aliyot, the Kaddish marks the end of the obligatory reading.[^tr17]
3. **When a second scroll is used.**  On Yom Tov, Shabbat Rosh Chodesh, Shabbat Chanukah, and the Four Parshiyot, the maftir is read from a second *sefer Torah*.[^tr18]  The common Ashkenazic order is:
    - Place the second scroll on the bimah next to the first, already rolled to the maftir reading.
    - The reader says half-Kaddish with both scrolls on the bimah.
    - Call *hagbah* and *gelilah* for the first scroll.
    - Call the maftir to the second scroll.

    When three scrolls are used, the maftir reads from the last one.[^tr19]  The common cases are:
    - Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Tevet during Chanukah (weekly portion, Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah).
    - Parashat Shekalim on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Adar (weekly portion, Rosh Chodesh, Shekalim), and likewise Parashat HaChodesh on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Nisan.
    - Simchat Torah (*Vezot Haberachah*, *Bereshit*, then maftir).

### The Announcement

The maftir is called with the standard formula, using the word *maftir* in place of the aliyah number:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] מַפְטִיר

If the maftir is a Kohen or Levi, add *ha-Kohen* or *ha-Levi* as for any other aliyah.  For a *bar mitzvah* boy many congregations announce:

יַעֲמֹד הַבָּחוּר הַבַּר מִצְוָה [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] מַפְטִיר

### Who Typically Receives Maftir

Maftir is customarily given to a *bar mitzvah* boy on the Shabbat of his bar mitzvah (see *Precedence Among Yisraelim* under Laws of Aliyot).  A mourner for a parent customarily takes maftir during the twelve months of mourning.[^tr20]  Many communities also give maftir to someone observing a *yahrzeit*.

Because the maftir also reads the haftarah, the gabbai must not, *lechatchilah*, call someone who cannot say it.  If such a person was called, another may say the haftarah in his place.[^tr21]

A minor may be called for maftir, but Ashkenazic custom avoids calling a minor for maftir, at least *lechatchilah*, for Parashat Zachor and Parashat Parah, on the first day of Shavuot, on the seventh day of Pesach, and on Shabbat Shuvah.[^tr22]

After the maftir reading, the second (or only) scroll is lifted and wrapped (see below).  The maftir does not begin the haftarah until the wrapping is finished, so that the *golel* can also listen.[^tr23]

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Sephardic communities perform *hagbah* *before* the reading rather than after it.[^tr24]  So only the wrapping and the return of the scroll remain to be completed before the haftarah begins.
:::

## Correcting Errors

The gabbai must follow the reading carefully in a *tikkun* (a printed book with both the vowelised and unvowelised text side by side) and correct any significant error (*taut*) by the reader (*ba'al korei*).

An error is considered significant (*me'akev*) if it changes the meaning of the text.  Minor mispronunciations that do not affect meaning need not be corrected publicly, though the gabbai may quietly guide the reader.
