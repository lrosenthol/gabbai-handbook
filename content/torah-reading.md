# Torah Reading / קְרִיאַת הַתּוֹרָה {#torah-reading}

## Overview

The public reading of the Torah (*keriat ha-Torah*) is performed from a handwritten parchment scroll (*sefer Torah*).  The gabbai is responsible for coordinating every aspect of the reading, from removing the scroll from the ark to its return.

This chapter follows the reading in the order of the service.  The laws behind it are collected in the two annexes at the back of the book: [Annex A: Laws of Aliyot](#annex-a) and [Annex B: Laws of the Gabbai](#annex-b).

## The Number of Aliyot (עֲלִיּוֹת) {#number-of-aliyot}

The number of Torah portions (*aliyot*) called varies by day. The following table documents the standard number of aliyot for the day/category.  It also notes (with +1) when the extra maftir/מַפְטִיר reading is added (for the person who will read the haftarah/הַפְטָרָה).  A gabbai may also call additional aliyot under certain circumstances ([TBD]).

| Day                                  | Number of *Aliyot* |
| ------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Monday / Thursday / Shabbat Mincha   | 3                  |
| Shabbat Morning                      | 7 + 1              |
| Rosh Chodesh / Chol Ha-Moed          | 4                  |
| Rosh Chodesh on Shabbat              | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Tov (three Pilgrimage Festivals) | 5 + 1              |
| Yom Tov on Shabbat                   | 7 + 1              |
| Rosh Hashanah                        | 5 + 1              |
| Rosh Hashanah on Shabbat             | 7 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur (Morning)                 | 6 + 1              |
| Yom Kippur (Afternoon)               | 2 + 1              |
| Fast Days (Morning)                  | 3                  |
| Fast Days (Afternoon)                | 2 + 1              |

NOTE: Fast Days refer to the public fasts: Tzom Gedaliah, Asarah b'Tevet, Ta'anit Esther, Shiv'ah Asar b'Tammuz and Tisha B'Av.

## Calling an *Oleh* {#calling-an-oleh}

When calling a person to the Torah, the gabbai announces him in Hebrew by his own Hebrew name and his father's Hebrew name, joined by *ben* ("son of").  Every call opens with *ya'amod* ("let him stand"); only the ending changes.  All of the calling formulas are collected in this section, and the sections that follow refer back to them.

Which name to use for a convert, or for someone whose father is unknown, is covered under [Names Used When Calling an Oleh](#names-in-announcement) in Annex A.  Learning the names before the service is one of the gabbai's duties (see [Knowing the Names](#knowing-the-names) in Annex B).

### The Numbered Aliyot {#calling-numbered-aliyot}

The first aliyah is announced by the oleh's status as a Kohen, with no number:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַכֹּהֵן

The second is announced by his status as a Levi:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַלֵּוִי

From the third aliyah onward, the gabbai announces the number of the aliyah:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] [שְׁלִישִׁי / רְבִיעִי / חֲמִישִׁי / שִׁשִּׁי / שְׁבִיעִי]

If a Kohen or Levi is called for a later aliyah (for example, *acharon* or maftir), the gabbai adds *ha-Kohen* or *ha-Levi* after his name, before the number.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Many Sephardic communities use the formula *ya'amod* followed only by the person's Hebrew name, without announcing the ordinal number of the aliyah.  The aliyah number is considered already known to the congregation.  Some communities also add the phrase *v'yevarech* ("and may he bless").[^tr1]
:::

::: note-chabad
**Chabad custom:** Chabad congregations call the oleh with the wording printed in the Chabad siddur.  Where it differs from the formulas above, the siddur's wording is followed.[^tr2]
:::

### When There Is No Kohen or Levi {#calling-no-kohen-levi}

When there is no Kohen, the first oleh (a Yisrael or a Levi) is announced *in place of a Kohen* (*bimkom Kohen*):

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] בִּמְקוֹם כֹּהֵן

Many gabbaim first say *ein kan Kohen* (אֵין כָּאן כֹּהֵן, "there is no Kohen here").  This is a common custom, not a requirement.  The second oleh is then a Yisrael, announced with the number *sheni* (שֵׁנִי).

When there is a Kohen but no Levi, the same Kohen is called again for the second aliyah, *in place of a Levi* (*bimkom Levi*):

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] הַכֹּהֵן בִּמְקוֹם לֵוִי

For when each of these applies, see [When a Kohen or Levi Isn't Present](#no-kohen-or-levi).

### Maftir {#calling-maftir}

The maftir is called with the word *maftir* in place of the aliyah number:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] מַפְטִיר

If the maftir is a Kohen or Levi, add *ha-Kohen* or *ha-Levi* after his name, as for any other aliyah.

### A Bar Mitzvah {#calling-bar-mitzvah}

For a *bar mitzvah* boy many congregations add *ha-bachur ha-bar mitzvah* before his name.  Called for maftir, he is announced:

יַעֲמֹד הַבָּחוּר הַבַּר מִצְוָה [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] מַפְטִיר

### Hagbah and Gelilah {#calling-hagbah-gelilah}

After the reading, one person is called to lift the scroll (*hagbahah*) and another to roll and dress it (*gelilah*):

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לְהַגְבָּהָה

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לִגְלִילָה

The gabbai usually calls both people together, so the golel is already at the bimah when the scroll is lifted.  If the congregation recites a *mi sheberach* for them, see the hagbahah / gelilah texts in the [Mishebairach](#mi-sheberach) chapter.

When more than one scroll is read, each scroll has its own pair (see [When More Than One Torah Is Read](#multiple-scrolls)).  When calling hagbah and gelilah for a second or third scroll, some gabbaim name which scroll it is so there is no confusion:

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לְהַגְבָּהַת סֵפֶר תּוֹרָה שֵׁנִי

יַעֲמֹד [שֵׁם] בֶּן [שֵׁם הָאָב] לִגְלִילַת סֵפֶר תּוֹרָה שֵׁנִי

For the third scroll, say שְׁלִישִׁי in place of שֵׁנִי.

## When a Kohen or Levi Isn't Present {#no-kohen-or-levi}

Normally the first aliyah goes to a *Kohen* and the second to a *Levi* (see [Kohen, Levi, and Yisrael](#kohen-levi-yisrael) in Annex A).  Before the Torah is taken out, the gabbai should look around the room to see who is there, so the first two aliyot can be planned before the reading starts.

### No Kohen Present

If there is no Kohen in the synagogue, the usual order no longer applies (*nitperdah ha-chavilah*, "the bundle has come apart").  A *Yisrael* is called for the first aliyah *in place of a Kohen* (*bimkom Kohen*).[^tr3]  A Levi may be called first instead, unless a Yisrael of greater standing is present, in which case the Yisrael is called.[^tr4]  Whoever is called, the gabbai announces that he is coming up *bimkom Kohen*, so that no one mistakes him for a Kohen.[^tr5]  For the wording, see [When There Is No Kohen or Levi](#calling-no-kohen-levi).

Once the first aliyah has gone to someone other than a Kohen, **a Levi is not called for the second aliyah**.  Otherwise people, especially anyone who arrived after the announcement, would assume the first oleh was a Kohen.  The second aliyah goes to a Yisrael and is announced as *sheni*.[^tr6]  In the same way, if a Levi was called first, another Levi must not be called second.[^tr7]  Authorities disagree about calling a Levi for a later aliyah (third or fourth) after a Yisrael was called *bimkom Kohen*.  Where the custom is to call Kohanim and Leviim only for *acharon* or *maftir*, the question doesn't come up.[^tr4]

### No Levi Present

If a Kohen is present but there is no Levi, **the Kohen who received the first aliyah is called again for the second**, *in place of a Levi* (*bimkom Levi*).  He recites both blessings again.[^tr8]  For the wording, see [When There Is No Kohen or Levi](#calling-no-kohen-levi).

A different Kohen is never called for the second aliyah, and a Yisrael is not called in place of the Levi.  Either would suggest that the first Kohen is not a proper Kohen.[^tr9]  This applies even on a fast day when the Levi who is present is not fasting.  The Kohen repeats *bimkom Levi* rather than a Yisrael being called.[^tr10]  The third aliyah onward goes to Yisraelim as usual.

### A Kohen Who Is Present but Unavailable

If the only Kohen present is reciting *Keriat Shema*, its blessings, or the *Amidah*, and may not interrupt, a Yisrael is called in his place.  The gabbai does not wait for him, and does not even delay taking out the Torah.[^tr11]  If the Kohen is between paragraphs (*bein ha-perakim*) and there is no other Kohen, he may be called.[^tr12]

On a public fast day, a Kohen who is not fasting is not called.  A Yisrael is called instead, and ideally the Kohen leaves the room so no one wonders why he was passed over.[^tr13]

If the gabbai wants to give the first aliyah to someone else while a Kohen is in the room, the Kohen must first waive his right or leave the room (see [Kohen, Levi, and Yisrael](#kohen-levi-yisrael) in Annex A).

### A Kohen Who Arrives Late

If a Kohen walks in after a Yisrael was called for the first aliyah:

- **If the Yisrael has already begun the blessing** (he has said *Baruch atah Hashem*), he continues.  His aliyah counts, and the Kohen is not called after him.[^tr14]
- **If only *Barchu* has been said**, the Yisrael steps aside and waits at the bimah while the Kohen and then a Levi are called.  The Yisrael then takes his aliyah.[^tr15]

### A Kohen or Levi Called Who Isn't There

If the gabbai calls a Kohen or Levi by name and he turns out not to be present, the gabbai does not call another Kohen (or Levi) by name, because that would suggest something is wrong with the first one.  The custom is for another Kohen or Levi to come up on his own.[^tr16]

### A Congregation of Only Kohanim (or Leviim)

If everyone present is a Kohen, Kohanim are called one after another.  No one draws any conclusion from it, since everyone knows only Kohanim are there.  If there is a single Yisrael among them, he is called first for the sake of peace (*darkhei shalom*).  The same applies to a congregation made up entirely of Leviim.[^tr17]

### Summary

| Who is present         | First aliyah                      | Second aliyah             |
| ---------------------- | --------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| Kohen and Levi         | Kohen                             | Levi                      |
| Kohen, no Levi         | Kohen                             | Same Kohen, *bimkom Levi* |
| Levi, no Kohen         | Yisrael or Levi, *bimkom Kohen*   | Yisrael (not a Levi)      |
| Neither Kohen nor Levi | Yisrael, *bimkom Kohen*           | Yisrael                   |

## Calling the Maftir (מַפְטִיר) {#maftir}

The *maftir* is the person called to the final, additional reading, who then reads (or chants) the *haftarah*/הַפְטָרָה from the Prophets.  For who may receive maftir and who customarily does, see [Laws of Maftir](#laws-of-maftir) in Annex A.

### When There Is a Maftir

A separate maftir is called on Shabbat morning, Yom Tov, Rosh Hashanah, and Yom Kippur morning, i.e., whenever a haftarah follows the reading.  At *Mincha* on a public fast day (see the list under [The Number of Aliyot](#number-of-aliyot)), at Yom Kippur *Mincha*, and on Tisha B'Av morning, there is no extra aliyah.  The third oleh is the maftir and reads the haftarah after his aliyah.[^tr18]

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** The haftarah at fast-day *Mincha* comes from the *Rama*'s gloss.  The Shulchan Aruch itself mentions only the Torah reading.  Many Sephardic communities therefore read no haftarah at *Mincha* on an ordinary fast day.  At such a reading the third oleh is not a maftir.  Follow the established custom of the congregation.
:::

### Before Calling the Maftir

1. **Confirm the maftir before the service.**  Make sure he knows which haftarah is being read (watch for special haftarot on Rosh Chodesh, the Four Parshiyot, and other special Shabbatot) and that he is prepared to read it and say its blessings.
2. **Half-Kaddish (*chatzi kaddish*).**  After the last required aliyah ends, the reader says half-Kaddish, and only then is the maftir called.  Because the maftir is not one of the required aliyot, the Kaddish marks the end of the obligatory reading.[^tr19]
3. **When a second scroll is used.**  On Yom Tov, Shabbat Rosh Chodesh, Shabbat Chanukah, and the Four Parshiyot, the maftir is read from a second *sefer Torah*.[^tr20]  When three scrolls are used, the maftir reads from the last one.[^tr21]  For which days need two or three scrolls, and the order of kaddish, hagbah and gelilah with each, see [When More Than One Torah Is Read](#multiple-scrolls) below.
4. **Call the maftir.**  For the wording, see [Maftir](#calling-maftir) and [A Bar Mitzvah](#calling-bar-mitzvah) under Calling an Oleh.

### After the Maftir Reading {#after-maftir}

After the maftir reading, the second (or only) scroll is lifted and wrapped (see [Hagbah and Gelilah](#hagbah-gelilah) below).  The maftir does not begin the haftarah until the wrapping is finished, so that the *golel* can also listen.[^tr22]

## Hagbah and Gelilah {#hagbah-gelilah}

After completing the final aliyah (including maftir), the torah is raised and then "redressed".  The people that do this job are called the Hagbah (the lifter) and the Golel (wrapper).  For the wording used to call them, see [Hagbah and Gelilah](#calling-hagbah-gelilah) under Calling an Oleh.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** In Sephardic communities (and some others) hagbahah is done *before* the reading, right after the scroll is placed on the bimah, rather than after it.  In many Sephardic communities the scroll is kept in a hard case (*tik*) and lifted standing up inside it.  The gabbai calls the honour at that point instead of after maftir, so after the maftir reading only the wrapping and the return of the scroll remain before the haftarah begins.[^tr23]
:::

### Hagbah (the lifter)

The magbiah opens the scroll about three columns wide (more if he can lift it safely).  He lifts it high and turns to his right and left, and to the front and back, so that the whole congregation can see the writing.[^tr24]  The congregation stands and says:

וְזֹאת הַתּוֹרָה אֲשֶׁר שָׂם מֹשֶׁה לִפְנֵי בְּנֵי יִשְׂרָאֵל עַל פִּי ה' בְּיַד מֹשֶׁה

Many also add:

תּוֹרַת ה' תְּמִימָה מְשִׁיבַת נָפֶשׁ

Lifting a full, open scroll takes strength and practice.  The gabbai should only give hagbah to someone who can do it safely and is comfortable doing it.  The gabbai should not give hagbah to someone whose hands shake badly, or to someone elderly or weak who would have to sit down right away instead of holding the scroll up long enough for the congregation to see it.  Such a person should also decline the honour himself.[^tr25]  When the scroll is heavy or most of the parchment is wound on one side (for example, in Bereishit or late in Devarim), the gabbai should choose someone with experience.

The magbiah then sits down holding the scroll, and the golel rolls it closed.

### Golel (the wrapper)

The golel rolls the scroll closed and dresses it: the binder (*gartel*), the mantle, and then the breastplate, pointer (*yad*) and crown as the community does it.  The scroll should be rolled tight and closed so that the two rollers meet at a seam between two sheets of parchment.  That way, if it ever tears, it tears at the seam.[^tr26]  The Talmud teaches that the golel receives a reward equal to that of all who read from the Torah.[^tr27]

Today one person lifts and another rolls, so the *magbiah* is considered the main golel.[^tr28]  This is one reason many communities treat hagbah as the more important of the two honours.

In many communities gelilah is given to a boy under bar mitzvah, to train him in mitzvot (*chinuch*).  The boy should be old enough to understand that he is handling something holy.[^tr25]  An adult should stand nearby to help, especially with a heavy scroll.

## When More Than One Torah Is Read {#multiple-scrolls}

On some days the reading comes from two or three different places in the Torah.  The scroll is not rolled from one place to another in front of the congregation, out of respect for the congregation (*kevod ha-tzibbur*).  So a separate scroll is prepared for each reading.  If the community has only one kosher scroll (for example, a small minyan, or a second scroll found to be *pasul*), the one scroll is rolled to the second reading in front of the congregation.[^tr29]  Each scroll gets its own hagbah and its own golel, so the gabbai needs to arrange **two or three pairs** of people before the service.

| Number of Scrolls | Days                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 2                 | Yom Tov (Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret); Rosh Hashanah; Yom Kippur morning; Shabbat Chol Ha-Moed; Chol Ha-Moed Pesach (weekdays); Shabbat Rosh Chodesh; Shabbat Chanukah; Rosh Chodesh Tevet (weekday, during Chanukah); Shabbat Zachor and Shabbat Parah; Shabbat Shekalim and Shabbat Ha-Chodesh (when not on Rosh Chodesh) |
| 3                 | Simchat Torah; Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Tevet (Chanukah); Shekalim on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Adar; Ha-Chodesh on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh Nissan                                                |

Note that weekday Chol Ha-Moed **Sukkot** uses only one scroll, unlike Chol Ha-Moed Pesach.

**Prepare every scroll before the service.**  Each scroll should be rolled to its reading ahead of time, and it should be clearly marked which scroll is first, second and third.  The gabbai should check this before the service, since a wrong scroll means rolling in front of the congregation.  He should also tell each magbiah and golel *before the service* which scroll is theirs.  This helps the reading go smoothly.

### Order with two scrolls

1. The main aliyot are read from the first scroll.
2. The second scroll is brought and placed on the bimah next to the first, already rolled to its reading.  The first scroll is not taken away until the second is on the bimah, so that no one's attention is taken off the mitzvah.[^tr30]
3. Half-kaddish is recited, with both scrolls on the bimah.[^tr31]
4. Hagbah and gelilah of the **first** scroll.  The magbiah sits holding it.  The second scroll is not opened, and its cover is not removed, until the first has been rolled.[^tr32]
5. Maftir (or the extra reading) is read from the second scroll.
6. Hagbah and gelilah of the **second** scroll.  Then the haftarah, once the golel has finished (see [After the Maftir Reading](#after-maftir)).

### Order with three scrolls

1. The main aliyot are read from the first scroll.
2. The second scroll is placed on the bimah.  **No kaddish is said yet.**
3. Hagbah and gelilah of the first scroll.
4. The next aliyah is read from the second scroll.
5. The third scroll is placed on the bimah next to the second, and half-kaddish is recited over these two.  The first scroll does not need to be brought back.[^tr31]
6. Hagbah and gelilah of the second scroll.
7. Maftir is read from the third scroll.
8. Hagbah and gelilah of the third scroll.  Then the haftarah.

On Simchat Torah, the order is often different, depending on local custom (many aliyot from the first scroll, *Chatan Torah*, *Chatan Bereishit* and maftir).  The gabbai should agree the plan with the rabbi ahead of time.

## Correcting Errors {#correcting-errors}

The gabbai must follow the reading carefully in a *tikkun* (a printed book with both the vowelised and unvowelised text side by side) and correct the reader (*ba'al korei*) when a significant error (*taut me'akev*) occurs.[^tr33]

An error is significant (*me'akev*) if it changes the meaning of the text.  The gabbai must know the text well enough to tell such an error from a minor mispronunciation.  Minor mispronunciations that do not affect meaning need not be corrected publicly, though the gabbai may quietly guide the reader.

If the gabbai himself makes an error (for instance, failing to correct a mistake that should have been corrected), the congregation should inform him.[^tr34]
