# Haftarah Blessings / בִּרְכוֹת הַהַפְטָרָה

## Overview

The maftir says one blessing before the haftarah and a set of blessings after it.[^hb1]  The blessing before the haftarah is the same every time.  The blessings after it change with the day:

- **The first three blessings after the haftarah** (*Tzur kol ha-olamim*, *Rachem al Tziyon* and *Samchenu*) are the same every time.
- **The fourth blessing** (*Al ha-Torah*) mentions the day.  There is one form for Shabbat, one for Yom Tov, one for Rosh Hashanah and one for Yom Kippur, and each of these has a Shabbat form.
- **On a weekday** (fast-day *Mincha*, Tisha B'Av morning) and at Yom Kippur *Mincha*, the fourth blessing is left out.  The maftir ends with *Magen David*.[^hb2]

The congregation answers *Amen* after each blessing.  Many maftirim do not know which form to use, especially when Yom Tov falls on Shabbat.  The gabbai should check before the service that the maftir knows which form is needed and that the correct text is open in front of him.

The colours used below are:

| Colour | Use                                      |
| ------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Black  | Every day / weekdays                     |
| Red    | Shabbat                                  |
| Green  | Yom Tov (Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret) |
| Purple | Yom Tov on Shabbat                       |
| Yellow | Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur             |

## Which Form to Use

| Day                                                      | After the haftarah                  |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Shabbat (including Shabbat Rosh Chodesh, Shabbat Chanukah, the Four Parshiyot, Shabbat Shuvah) | Three blessings + Shabbat           |
| Shabbat Chol Ha-Moed (Pesach and Sukkot)                 | Three blessings + Shabbat           |
| Yom Tov (Pesach, Shavuot, Sukkot, Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah) | Three blessings + Yom Tov           |
| Yom Tov on Shabbat                                       | Three blessings + Yom Tov on Shabbat |
| Rosh Hashanah                                            | Three blessings + Rosh Hashanah     |
| Rosh Hashanah on Shabbat                                 | Three blessings + Rosh Hashanah on Shabbat |
| Yom Kippur (morning)                                     | Three blessings + Yom Kippur        |
| Yom Kippur on Shabbat (morning)                          | Three blessings + Yom Kippur on Shabbat |
| Yom Kippur (*Mincha*), on a weekday or on Shabbat        | Three blessings only                |
| Public fast days (*Mincha*) and Tisha B'Av (morning and *Mincha*) | Three blessings only                |

**Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah and Chol Ha-Moed on Shabbat are not mentioned.**  The maftir uses the regular Shabbat form and ends *mekadesh ha-Shabbat*.  Only Yom Tov itself (and Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur) is mentioned in the haftarah blessing.[^hb3]

## The Blessing Before the Haftarah

Said on every day a haftarah is read.

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר בָּחַר בִּנְבִיאִים טוֹבִים, וְרָצָה בְדִבְרֵיהֶם הַנֶּאֱמָרִים בֶּאֱמֶת. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', הַבּוֹחֵר בַּתּוֹרָה וּבְמֹשֶׁה עַבְדּוֹ, וּבְיִשְׂרָאֵל עַמּוֹ, וּבִנְבִיאֵי הָאֱמֶת וָצֶדֶק.
:::

## The Blessings After the Haftarah

### The First Three Blessings

Said on every day a haftarah is read.  On a weekday and at Yom Kippur *Mincha*, the maftir stops after *Magen David*.

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, צוּר כָּל הָעוֹלָמִים, צַדִּיק בְּכָל הַדּוֹרוֹת, הָאֵל הַנֶּאֱמָן, הָאוֹמֵר וְעֹשֶׂה, הַמְדַבֵּר וּמְקַיֵּם, שֶׁכָּל דְּבָרָיו אֱמֶת וָצֶדֶק. נֶאֱמָן אַתָּה הוּא ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, וְנֶאֱמָנִים דְּבָרֶיךָ, וְדָבָר אֶחָד מִדְּבָרֶיךָ אָחוֹר לֹא יָשׁוּב רֵיקָם, כִּי אֵל מֶלֶךְ נֶאֱמָן וְרַחֲמָן אָתָּה. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', הָאֵל הַנֶּאֱמָן בְּכָל דְּבָרָיו.

רַחֵם עַל צִיּוֹן כִּי הִיא בֵּית חַיֵּינוּ, וְלַעֲלוּבַת נֶפֶשׁ תּוֹשִׁיעַ בִּמְהֵרָה בְיָמֵינוּ. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', מְשַׂמֵּחַ צִיּוֹן בְּבָנֶיהָ.

שַׂמְּחֵנוּ ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ בְּאֵלִיָּהוּ הַנָּבִיא עַבְדֶּךָ, וּבְמַלְכוּת בֵּית דָּוִד מְשִׁיחֶךָ, בִּמְהֵרָה יָבֹא וְיָגֵל לִבֵּנוּ. עַל כִּסְאוֹ לֹא יֵשֶׁב זָר, וְלֹא יִנְחֲלוּ עוֹד אֲחֵרִים אֶת כְּבוֹדוֹ, כִּי בְשֵׁם קָדְשְׁךָ נִשְׁבַּעְתָּ לּוֹ, שֶׁלֹּא יִכְבֶּה נֵרוֹ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', מָגֵן דָּוִד.
:::

### Fast Days and Tisha B'Av

On a public fast day (Tzom Gedaliah, Asarah b'Tevet, Ta'anit Esther, Shiv'ah Asar b'Tammuz) at *Mincha*, and on Tisha B'Av, the haftarah is read on a weekday.  The maftir says the blessing before the haftarah and the **first three blessings** after it, ending with *Magen David*.  There is no fourth blessing.[^hb2]

A public fast that would fall on Shabbat is moved to a weekday (for example, Tisha B'Av is postponed to Sunday), so the same rule applies.  The only fast whose haftarah can be read on Shabbat is Yom Kippur (below).

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Many Sephardic communities read no haftarah at *Mincha* on an ordinary fast day (see *When There Is a Maftir* in the *Torah Reading* chapter), so these blessings are said only on Tisha B'Av.  Follow the established custom of the congregation.
:::

### Yom Kippur Mincha

At Yom Kippur *Mincha* the haftarah is the Book of Jonah.  The maftir says the blessing before the haftarah and the **first three blessings** after it, ending with *Magen David*.  This is so even when Yom Kippur falls on Shabbat, since there is no haftarah at Shabbat *Mincha*.[^hb4]

### Shabbat

Also used on Shabbat Rosh Chodesh, Shabbat Chanukah, Shabbat Chol Ha-Moed, the Four Parshiyot and every other Shabbat that is not Yom Tov.

::: prayer-shabbat
עַל הַתּוֹרָה, וְעַל הָעֲבוֹדָה, וְעַל הַנְּבִיאִים, וְעַל יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת הַזֶּה, שֶׁנָּתַתָּ לָּנוּ ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, לִקְדֻשָּׁה וְלִמְנוּחָה, לְכָבוֹד וּלְתִפְאָרֶת. עַל הַכֹּל ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, אֲנַחְנוּ מוֹדִים לָךְ, וּמְבָרְכִים אוֹתָךְ, יִתְבָּרַךְ שִׁמְךָ בְּפִי כָּל חַי תָּמִיד לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', מְקַדֵּשׁ הַשַּׁבָּת.
:::

### Yom Tov

Said on the first and last days of Pesach, Shavuot, the first days of Sukkot, and Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah, when they fall on a weekday.  The name of the festival is inserted where marked:

| Festival                            | Insert                         |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Pesach                              | חַג הַמַּצּוֹת הַזֶּה             |
| Shavuot                             | חַג הַשָּׁבֻעוֹת הַזֶּה             |
| Sukkot                              | חַג הַסֻּכּוֹת הַזֶּה              |
| Shemini Atzeret / Simchat Torah     | הַשְּׁמִינִי חַג הָעֲצֶרֶת הַזֶּה      |

::: prayer-chaggim
עַל הַתּוֹרָה, וְעַל הָעֲבוֹדָה, וְעַל הַנְּבִיאִים, וְעַל יוֹם [חַג הַמַּצּוֹת / חַג הַשָּׁבֻעוֹת / חַג הַסֻּכּוֹת / הַשְּׁמִינִי חַג הָעֲצֶרֶת] הַזֶּה, שֶׁנָּתַתָּ לָּנוּ ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, לְשָׂשׂוֹן וּלְשִׂמְחָה, לְכָבוֹד וּלְתִפְאָרֶת. עַל הַכֹּל ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, אֲנַחְנוּ מוֹדִים לָךְ, וּמְבָרְכִים אוֹתָךְ, יִתְבָּרַךְ שִׁמְךָ בְּפִי כָּל חַי תָּמִיד לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', מְקַדֵּשׁ יִשְׂרָאֵל וְהַזְּמַנִּים.
:::

### Yom Tov on Shabbat

::: prayer-shabbat-chaggim
עַל הַתּוֹרָה, וְעַל הָעֲבוֹדָה, וְעַל הַנְּבִיאִים, וְעַל יוֹם הַשַּׁבָּת הַזֶּה, וְעַל יוֹם [חַג הַמַּצּוֹת / חַג הַשָּׁבֻעוֹת / חַג הַסֻּכּוֹת / הַשְּׁמִינִי חַג הָעֲצֶרֶת] הַזֶּה, שֶׁנָּתַתָּ לָּנוּ ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, לִקְדֻשָּׁה וְלִמְנוּחָה, לְשָׂשׂוֹן וּלְשִׂמְחָה, לְכָבוֹד וּלְתִפְאָרֶת. עַל הַכֹּל ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ, אֲנַחְנוּ מוֹדִים לָךְ, וּמְבָרְכִים אוֹתָךְ, יִתְבָּרַךְ שִׁמְךָ בְּפִי כָּל חַי תָּמִיד לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', מְקַדֵּשׁ הַשַּׁבָּת וְיִשְׂרָאֵל וְהַזְּמַנִּים.
:::

## Variations in Wording

The texts above follow the common Ashkenazic wording.  Siddurim and *machzorim* differ in small details.  For example, some omit *ve-rachaman* (וְרַחֲמָן) in the first blessing after the haftarah, and some add *malkenu* (מַלְכֵּנוּ) in *u-devarcha emet* on Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur.  None of these changes the form required for the day.  If the congregation uses a particular siddur, the maftir should follow its wording.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Sephardic siddurim have the same structure (one blessing before, three after, and a fourth that mentions the day), with small differences in wording.  Use the congregation's siddur.
:::
