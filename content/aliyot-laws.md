# Annex A: Laws of Aliyot / נִסְפָּח א: הֲלָכוֹת עֲלִיַּת הַתּוֹרָה {#annex-a}

This annex sets out the laws behind the practical chapter [Torah Reading](#torah-reading): who may be called, in what order, and how the oleh conducts himself.  The wording the gabbai uses to call each oleh is in [Calling an Oleh](#calling-an-oleh).

## Who Is Eligible for an Aliyah

Any male Jew who has reached the age of majority (*bar mitzvah*, thirteen years and one day) is eligible to receive an aliyah.[^al1] A minor may be called for *maftir* on Shabbat and Yom Tov in extenuating circumstances, but not for a numbered weekday aliyah.[^al2]

A person who is deaf-mute (*cheresh*) in both hearing and speech, or who lacks mental capacity (*shoteh*), may not receive an aliyah because they cannot recite the blessings meaningfully.[^al3] One who is deaf but can speak, or mute but can hear, follows a lenient ruling in most communities.[^al4]

### Kohen, Levi, and Yisrael {#kohen-levi-yisrael}

The first aliyah is given to a *Kohen*, the second to a *Levi*, and all remaining aliyot to *Yisraelim*.[^al5] This order maintains the honour of the Torah and reflects the biblical distinctions of the priestly and levitical classes.

If no Kohen or no Levi is present, the order changes; see [When a Kohen or Levi Isn't Present](#no-kohen-or-levi).

If a Kohen is present but the gabbai wishes to call a non-Kohen (e.g., for a *bar mitzvah* boy who is a Yisrael), the Kohen must be formally asked to leave the synagogue or declare that he waives his precedence (*mechilah*) before the first aliyah is called.[^al6]

::: note-chabad
**Chabad custom (Lubavitch):** The Rebbe (Rabbi Menachem Mendel Schneerson) ruled that it is preferable that the Kohen himself declare his waiver (*mechilah*) aloud before the first aliyah is called, and that asking him to leave the room is not the preferred method. The Kohen remains present but explicitly yields precedence.[^al7]
:::

### Precedence Among Yisraelim {#precedence}

Among Yisraelim, the following individuals have precedence for receiving an aliyah, listed from highest to lowest:

1. A *bar mitzvah* boy on the Shabbat of his bar mitzvah[^al8]
2. A bridegroom (*chatan*) on the Shabbat before or after his wedding[^al9]
3. A mourner (*avel*) within the first year of bereavement — custom varies[^al10]
4. A father whose son is to be circumcised (*brit milah*) that week[^al11]
5. A person observing a *yahrzeit*[^al12]
6. A person who has just returned from a journey at sea or across the desert, recovered from a serious illness, or been released from captivity (*bentching gomel*)[^al13]

These precedences are social conventions (*minhag*) and may be adjusted by the gabbai to maintain communal peace (*shalom ha-tzibbur*).

## Disqualified Individuals

### Public Violations (*Mechalel Shabbat Befarhesya*)

One who publicly desecrates the Shabbat — meaning regularly and openly, in a manner known to the community — should not be called to the Torah according to many *poskim*.[^al14] This is a sensitive matter and the gabbai should consult the rabbi of the community for guidance rather than act unilaterally.

### Apostates and Non-Jews

An apostate (*meshumad*) who has converted to another religion is not called to the Torah.[^al15] A non-Jew may not receive an aliyah under any circumstances.[^al16]

### A Person Under *Cherem*

One placed under communal ban (*cherem*) should not receive an aliyah until the ban is lifted.[^al17]

## Names Used When Calling an Oleh {#names-in-announcement}

The oleh is called by his Hebrew name and his father's Hebrew name.[^al18]  For the announcement formulas themselves, see [Calling an Oleh](#calling-an-oleh).

### When the Father's Name Is Not Used

Under normal circumstances the father's name is used. A foundling (*asufi*) or a person whose father is unknown (*shetuki*) is called by the name of his maternal grandfather; if that too is unknown, he is called בֶּן אַבְרָהָם, as a convert is.[^al19]

A convert (*ger*) is called בֶּן אַבְרָהָם (son of Abraham), since Abraham is regarded as the spiritual father of all converts.[^al20] Some communities use the fuller form בֶּן אַבְרָהָם אָבִינוּ (son of Abraham our Father), which is the formula the Shulchan Aruch prescribes for a convert's name in a *get*.[^al21]

## Laws of Maftir {#laws-of-maftir}

Maftir is treated as a separate aliyah. The maftir section slightly overlaps with the final aliyah (*shevi'i* on Shabbat), repeating the last several verses.[^al22] This was instituted so that those called for maftir would not appear to be receiving a lesser honour.[^al23]

A Kohen or Levi may receive maftir without displacing the regular sequence, since maftir is an addition rather than one of the seven required aliyot.[^al24]

For when a maftir is called and the order of the service around it, see [Calling the Maftir](#maftir).

### Who Typically Receives Maftir

Maftir is customarily given to a *bar mitzvah* boy on the Shabbat of his bar mitzvah (see [Precedence Among Yisraelim](#precedence)).  A mourner for a parent customarily takes maftir during the twelve months of mourning.[^al25]  Many communities also give maftir to someone observing a *yahrzeit*.

Because the maftir also reads the haftarah, the gabbai must not, *lechatchilah*, call someone who cannot say it.  If such a person was called, another may say the haftarah in his place.[^al26]

A minor may be called for maftir, but Ashkenazic custom avoids calling a minor for maftir, at least *lechatchilah*, for Parashat Zachor and Parashat Parah, on the first day of Shavuot, on the seventh day of Pesach, and on Shabbat Shuvah.[^al27]

## The Aliyah Itself {#oleh-conduct}

### Approaching the Torah

The oleh should approach the *bimah* promptly.[^al28] He should take the shorter path up and the longer path down, to show eagerness to perform the mitzvah.[^al29] Some authorities require him to use a gartel (sash) if he is not already wearing one.[^al30]

### Looking at the Place in the Scroll

Before reciting the blessing, the oleh opens the scroll and looks at the place where reading will begin. He should not begin the blessing until he has located the text.[^al31]

### Reciting the Blessings

The oleh recites the blessing before the reading (*brachah rishonah*) and the blessing after the reading (*brachah achronah*). He must recite them aloud, and the congregation responds *Amen*.[^al32]

If the oleh began reciting the blessing before the scroll was pointed to the correct place, he does not repeat the blessing.[^al33]

### Remaining at the Bimah

After his portion is read, the oleh must remain standing at the bimah until the next oleh has finished his opening blessing.[^al34] He should not return to his seat until the next oleh's blessing is complete, as it would appear as though he is walking away from the Torah disrespectfully.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Many Sephardic communities permit the oleh to return to his seat immediately after completing his closing blessing, before the next person comes up. This is the ruling of the *Ben Ish Chai*.[^al35]
:::

### Holding the Torah

The oleh holds the two handles (*atzei chayyim*) of the scroll during the blessings. He should not hold the parchment itself with bare hands.[^al36]

## Special Situations

### A Person Who Cannot Read Hebrew

An oleh who cannot follow the reading is still permitted to receive an aliyah, provided he recites the blessings correctly. The ba'al korei (Torah reader) reads on his behalf.[^al37] However, *lechatchilah* (ideally) one should not call someone who cannot follow along at all, if it can be avoided.[^al38]

### Two Brothers or a Father and Son in Succession

Two brothers, or a father and son, should not receive consecutive aliyot, to avoid the *ayin hara* (evil eye). Some authorities extend this to any two close relatives.[^al39] In small communities where this is unavoidable, the custom is more lenient.[^al40]

### Skipping an Aliyah

The gabbai should not skip aliyot — for example, calling a third and fifth aliyah while omitting the fourth. If an aliyah was accidentally skipped and not noticed until later, the readings already performed are not invalidated, but the skipped aliyah must be made up if possible.[^al41]
