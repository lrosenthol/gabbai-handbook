# Torah and Gomel Blessings / בִּרְכוֹת הַתּוֹרָה וְהַגּוֹמֵל {#torah-and-gomel-blessings}

## The Aliyah Blessings / בִּרְכוֹת הַעֲלִיָּה

Every person called for an aliyah says a blessing before and after his assigned Torah reading.  The oleh opens or locates the scroll, holds both handles (*atzim*), and says the blessing aloud.  The congregation answers *Amen*; after the opening formula, it answers the opening response and the oleh repeats it.[^tgb1]

The transliterations below follow a common Ashkenazic pronunciation.  Pronunciation varies among communities; follow the congregation's siddur and custom.

### Before the Reading

The gabbai or congregation does not say *Barchu* instead of the opening formula; the oleh must invite the congregation:

::: prayer
בָּרְכוּ אֶת ה' הַמְבֹרָךְ.
:::

*Bar'chu et Adonai ha-mevorach.*

The congregation answers:

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ ה' הַמְבֹרָךְ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד.
:::

*Baruch Adonai ha-mevorach le'olam va'ed.*

The oleh repeats the congregation's response, then continues:

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ ה' הַמְבֹרָךְ לְעוֹלָם וָעֶד.

בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר בָּחַר־בָּנוּ מִכָּל הָעַמִּים, וְנָתַן־לָנוּ אֶת־תּוֹרָתוֹ. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', נוֹתֵן הַתּוֹרָה.
:::

*Baruch Adonai ha-mevorach le'olam va'ed.*

*Baruch atah Adonai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher bachar banu mikol ha'amim, ve-natan lanu et Torato. Baruch atah Adonai, noten ha-Torah.*

The blessing is said only after the oleh has opened the scroll and found the place where he is to begin.  He should hold at least one handle with both hands if possible.  If he began before the scroll was positioned correctly, he does not repeat the blessing.[^tgb2]

### After the Reading

After the reader or ba'al korei has completed the assigned passage and the scroll has been raised and covered according to the congregation's custom, the oleh says:

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, אֲשֶׁר נָתַן־לָנוּ תּוֹרַת אֶמֶת, וְחַיֵּי עוֹלָם נָטַע בְּתוֹכֵנוּ. בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה', נוֹתֵן הַתּוֹרָה.
:::

*Baruch atah Adonai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, asher natan lanu Torat emet, ve-chayei olam nata betocheinu. Baruch atah Adonai, noten ha-Torah.*

The oleh remains standing at the bimah until the next oleh has completed his opening blessing.  He should not walk away merely because his portion was read.

### When a Person Has Already Said the Torah Blessing

Someone who said the Torah blessing before coming to the bimah, for example during personal prayer, does not use that blessing to avoid the public blessing.  When he is called to the Torah, he says *Asher bachar banu* again, because it is recited for the public reading.[^tgb3]

Conversely, a person who was called to the Torah is excused from saying the personal morning Torah blessing that he would ordinarily have said first.  The public reading serves that purpose.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Communities commonly use the standard formula above and answer *Baruch H' ha-m'vorach l'olam va-ed*.  Some Sephardic siddurim write *eloheinu* or *Elokenu* in slightly different vocalizations, and some write *noten ha-Torah* as *notein ha-Torah*.  The meaning and structure are the same.  Use the exact nusach and version of the *H'* Name printed in the congregation's siddur.
:::

## Birkat HaGomel / בִּרְכַּת הַגּוֹמֵל

Four categories of people are traditionally obligated to recite the blessing of thanksgiving: one who crossed a desert or sea, one who recovered from a serious illness, and one released from prison.  Many authorities and Sephardic communities apply the sea and desert rules broadly when a journey involved danger; in an ordinary short journey or a routine illness, consult the congregation's rabbi.[^tgb4]

The blessing is recited publicly, preferably after the Torah reading, where a minyan is present.  In the usual Ashkenazic custom, the gabbai calls the person to an aliyah and he says *Birkat HaGomel* after the reading and his ordinary blessing.  The community then answers.

Before the Torah is read, the gabbai should make clear to the person that he will not simply say *Birkat HaGomel* on its own: he must receive an aliyah and recite the ordinary Torah blessings as well.

::: prayer
בָּרוּךְ אַתָּה ה' אֱלֹהֵינוּ מֶלֶךְ הָעוֹלָם, הַגּוֹמֵל לְחַיָּבִים טוֹבוֹת שֶׁגְּמָלַנִי כָּל טוֹב.
:::

*Baruch atah Adonai, Eloheinu melech ha'olam, ha-Gomel le-chayavim tovot, she-gemalani kol tov.*

The congregation answers:

::: prayer
מִי שֶׁגְּמָלְךָ טוֹב הוּא יִגְמָלְךָ טוֹב סֶלָה.
:::

*Mi she-gemalcha tov, hu yigmalcha kol tov sela.*

Some say *Migomel tov* or use *kol tov* instead of *tov*, according to their siddur.  The congregation should answer at an audible volume so that the person blessing can hear the response.

::: note-sephardic
**Sephardic custom:** Sephardic communities commonly recite *Birkat HaGomel* after the Torah reading and follow the wording of their own siddur.  Sephardic custom also generally requires the blessing for intercity travel, while Ashkenazic practice may be narrower.  Follow the congregation's established custom and the rabbi's ruling in a doubtful case.[^tgb5]
:::

If several people are saying *Gomel*, each may say it after his own aliyah, or the congregation may arrange for them to be called together.  If one person recited *Gomel* and another heard and intended to discharge his obligation, the latter may be covered if he also answered *Amen* or, according to some authorities, without *Amen*; consult the rabbi for a mixed or unusual situation.[^tgb6]

*Birkat HaGomel* should not be delayed unnecessarily.  Although authorities give it a period of three days for a reminder, or allow it throughout a person's life in some circumstances, the gabbai should encourage the person to do it at the next Torah reading while a minyan is present.[^tgb7]
